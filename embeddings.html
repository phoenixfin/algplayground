Embeddings — words as pointsEmbedding — kata sebagai titik

A neural network cannot read words, only numbers. An embedding turns each word into a short list of numbers (a point in space) so that words used in similar ways land close together. Here you build embeddings from a tiny text corpus yourself: count which words appear near each other, squeeze the counts into a few dimensions, then watch a small skip-gram network learn the same thing live. Everything runs in your browser.

Jaringan saraf tidak bisa membaca kata, hanya angka. Sebuah embedding mengubah tiap kata menjadi daftar pendek angka (sebuah titik di ruang) sehingga kata yang dipakai dengan cara serupa berada berdekatan. Di sini kamu membangun embedding dari korpus teks kecil: hitung kata mana yang muncul berdekatan, padatkan hitungan itu ke beberapa dimensi, lalu saksikan jaringan skip-gram kecil mempelajari hal yang sama secara langsung. Semuanya berjalan di browsermu.

You shall know a word by the company it keepsKenali sebuah kata dari teman-temannya

"King" and "queen" almost never appear in the same sentence here, yet both appear next to crown, palace and throne. Count, for every word, how often each other word appears within a few words of it. Each word's row of counts is already a crude vector. Two words with similar rows are used similarly.

"King" dan "queen" nyaris tak pernah muncul dalam kalimat yang sama di sini, namun keduanya muncul di dekat crown, palace, dan throne. Hitung, untuk setiap kata, seberapa sering kata lain muncul dalam beberapa kata di sekitarnya. Baris hitungan setiap kata sudah merupakan vektor kasar. Dua kata dengan baris yang mirip dipakai dengan cara yang mirip.

Why PPMI instead of raw counts? Frequent words like he co-occur with everything, which says little. Pointwise mutual information asks how much more often two words meet than chance would predict, $\mathrm{PMI}(w,c)=\log\frac{P(w,c)}{P(w)P(c)}$. We keep only positive values (PPMI).

Kenapa PPMI, bukan hitungan mentah? Kata sering seperti he muncul bersama semuanya, sehingga tak banyak artinya. Pointwise mutual information bertanya seberapa sering dua kata bertemu dibanding kebetulan, $\mathrm{PMI}(w,c)=\log\frac{P(w,c)}{P(w)P(c)}$. Kita hanya menyimpan nilai positif (PPMI).

Try this

  2. Select paris and france: they do co-occur directly, as well as sharing contexts with rome / italy.
  3. Untick drop stop-words: "the" and "of" flood the table and the PPMI bars change little, which is exactly what the log-ratio is for.
  4. Add a line such as the duke wore a crown and select duke: a brand-new word gets a meaningful row from a single repeated sentence.

Squeeze the table into a few dimensionsPadatkan tabel menjadi beberapa dimensi

The PPMI table has one column per vocabulary word, which is far too many numbers per word and mostly noise. A singular value decomposition (SVD) keeps only the d strongest directions of variation. Each word becomes d numbers; words with similar rows stay near each other. With d = 2 we can draw every word on paper.

Tabel PPMI punya satu kolom per kata kosakata, terlalu banyak angka per kata dan kebanyakan derau. Dekomposisi nilai singular (SVD) hanya mempertahankan d arah variasi terkuat. Tiap kata menjadi d angka; kata dengan baris mirip tetap berdekatan. Dengan d = 2 kita bisa menggambar setiap kata di atas kertas.

Directions carry meaningArah membawa makna

Nobody told the algorithm about gender or royalty, yet the step from man to woman points the same way as the step from king to queen. So king − man + woman lands near queen. Predict the neighbours; the vectors fall outPrediksi tetangga; vektornya muncul sendiri

Instead of counting, word2vec gives every word a random vector and trains a tiny network on one task: given a word, is this other word really one of its neighbours? Real neighbours are pulled together, random words are pushed away (negative sampling). After enough steps, words used alike end up near each other. Press Train and watch the map form.

Alih-alih menghitung, word2vec memberi tiap kata vektor acak dan melatih jaringan mungil pada satu tugas: diberi sebuah kata, apakah kata lain ini benar-benar tetangganya? Tetangga asli ditarik mendekat, kata acak didorong menjauh (negative sampling). Setelah cukup langkah, kata yang dipakai serupa berakhir berdekatan. Tekan Train dan saksikan peta terbentuk.

What this toy can and cannot doYang bisa dan tak bisa dilakukan mainan ini

Can: show that meaning can be a position, that similarity is an angle, and that relations become directions. Cannot: give one word several meanings (“bank” gets one vector, the average of all its uses), nor match real word2vec / GloVe quality, which need billions of words.

Bisa: menunjukkan bahwa makna bisa berupa posisi, kemiripan adalah sudut, dan relasi menjadi arah. Tak bisa: memberi satu kata beberapa makna (“bank” hanya punya satu vektor, rata-rata semua pemakaiannya), atau menyamai kualitas word2vec / GloVe asli yang butuh miliaran kata.

A word of cautionSebuah peringatan

Embeddings absorb whatever patterns the text contains, including stereotypes. Our corpus makes he go with king by construction. A web-scale corpus makes occupations drift towards gender the same way, without anyone intending it. That is why embeddings are audited, not trusted.

Embedding menyerap pola apa pun yang ada pada teks, termasuk stereotip. Korpus kita membuat he berpasangan dengan king secara sengaja. Korpus sebesar web membuat profesi condong ke gender tertentu dengan cara yang sama, tanpa ada yang menghendakinya. Karena itu embedding diaudit, bukan dipercaya begitu saja.
